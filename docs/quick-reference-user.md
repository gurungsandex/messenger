# Messenger — quick reference

Everything on one page. Your IT team runs this on your own company's server; messages never
leave your organisation's network.

---

## Signing in

If your computer is on the company domain, you are signed in automatically. Otherwise enter
your username and password.

Sign-in failures always say the same thing regardless of the cause — this is deliberate, so
that nobody can use the sign-in screen to work out which usernames exist. If you are stuck,
IT can see the specific reason.

After several wrong attempts there is a short wait that gets longer each time and then
clears on its own. Your account is not locked.

---

## Chatting

| Action | How |
| --- | --- |
| Start a 1:1 chat | Search for the person, click their name |
| Join a group chat | Groups are created by IT; yours appear automatically |
| Search history | `Ctrl+F` |
| Send a file | Drag onto the conversation, or the paperclip |

A **single tick** means the server has your message and it will be delivered. A **double
tick** means the recipient's app has it. If they are offline it waits and arrives when they
next sign in — nothing is lost.

**New to a group?** You will see messages from when you joined onward, not the discussion
that happened before. That is intentional.

---

## Your status

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Online** | Available |
| **Busy** | Available but do not disturb |
| **Away** | Set automatically after you stop using your computer, or manually |
| **Offline** | Signed out or disconnected |

Only people you share a conversation or group with can see your status.

---

## Files

Drag a file onto a conversation. Large files upload in pieces and **resume** if your
connection drops — you do not have to start again.

If your organisation has virus scanning enabled, a file becomes downloadable once it has
been scanned. A file that cannot be scanned is withheld rather than passed along.

Size limits and blocked file types are set by your organisation.

---

## Being signed out automatically

You are signed out after a period of inactivity, set by your organisation. This protects
your conversations if you leave your desk. Sign back in and your history is all still there.

---

## Common messages

| You see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Sign-in failed | Check username and password. Contact IT if it persists |
| **Cannot verify the server** | **Do not continue. Tell IT immediately** — it can indicate someone intercepting the connection |
| Connection lost | Reconnecting automatically; messages you sent are safe |
| File too large | Over your organisation's limit — share another way |
| File type not allowed | Blocked by policy |
| Still scanning | Virus scan in progress; it will become available |
| Session expired | Signed out for inactivity — sign back in |

## Privacy — worth knowing

Your organisation runs this server, and **administrators can access message and file
history**. That is how the system is built: it exists so your employer can meet legal and
compliance obligations such as records retention and eDiscovery.

This is **not** end-to-end encrypted messaging. Messages are encrypted while travelling
across the network and while stored, but your organisation holds the keys.

Treat it as you would company email: private from outsiders, not private from your employer.
